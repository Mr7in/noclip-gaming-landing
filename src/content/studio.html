How Fullcourt plays.

Every match is a short story the player writes: who starts, how the team attacks, where the money goes and what changes at the break. Backcheck plays the same way on ice.

  1. Build your team.

    Draft five from a 500-player league across 50 clubs, then open packs to make the squad stronger. Chemistry between club, nation and play style rewards smart building, not just big pulls.

    • Four card tiers, from Common to Legendary
    • Chemistry links drawn on the court

  2. Call the game.

    Pick an offence (Balanced, Pace and space, Post-up, Pick and roll, Grind) and a defence (Man-to-man, 2-3 zone, Full-court press, Switch everything, Deny their star). Tactics counter each other, and the AI coach adjusts at the break.

    • Win chance updates with every choice
    • Up to two boosters, priced into the odds

  3. Bet the match, then bet it again.

    Bets go on before tip-off and at every quarter break: match winner, handicap, total points, the captain's points and a bet builder. Change the plan at a break and every market re-prices on the spot.

    • Five markets, priced from simulations of this exact matchup
    • Cash out at every break
    • Stakes from €0.10, configurable by the operator

  4. Watch it play out.

    Every possession plays out in a 3D arena with an LED scoreboard and play-by-play. Under the court, the live bet tracker shows every open bet and where it stands right now: leading, trailing, or what it still needs.

    • Every open bet tracked live
    • Quarter breaks with the box score and fresh odds

Questions operators ask.

What is a skin?

A sport on top of the shared engine: its match rules and simulation, its league and players, its markets, and its art and sound. Odds, settlement, wallet, collection, season pass and operator switches are shared across titles.

Which sports can we play today?

Basketball (Fullcourt) and ice hockey (Backcheck) have playable demos. Backcourt is a lighter 2D presentation of the same basketball engine. Football and volleyball are next, and further sports follow operator demand.

Is it a real league?

It is a look-alike league: 30 US-city clubs and 20 European clubs with real cities, new nicknames and fictional players. Players recognise the names, and you carry no licence, data feed or integrity risk. Each league lives in its own data files, so an operator with rights could swap in licensed names later.

What return does the operator keep?

Default return to player is about 92% across all bets: match winner 94%, handicap and total points 94.5%, captain's points 92%, bet builder 88%. The margin is agreed with each operator. Squad strength changes the odds a player gets, not the house edge.

Can buying cards buy better betting returns?

No. A stronger squad gets shorter odds in exact proportion, so return stays the same. Features that could indirectly affect returns can be switched off per market.

Has a test lab checked the RNG yet?

Not yet. Matches and prices run on the server with a cryptographically secure RNG, and the engine and its RTP audit are certification-ready for lab testing ahead of a regulated launch.

What markets are offered?

Match winner, handicap, total points, the captain's points over/under and a bet builder, before tip-off and at every break.

What stake range is supported?

€0.10 to €500 per bet by default, configurable higher by the operator.

How long is a match?

Two to five minutes of play, with speed up to 4x and a skip to each break.

Do players have to bet to play?

No. Every match is playable and rewarding without a stake. Betting is an optional layer on top.

Can the game carry our brand?

Yes. Your colours, logo, type, arena branding, card art and league naming, up to a fully custom league. Gameplay and maths stay the same in every skin.

Where should it live in our lobby?

On its own page in your main navigation, next to Sports, with a tile in the sports lobby. Not inside the slots grid: the audience is your sportsbook players.

Can we start small?

Yes. Launch in one market with the features you choose, measure repeat play and turnover per player, and widen from there.
